# Banking-process glossary

This glossary uses common process-design language for the illustrative diagrams in this repository. Terms can have different meanings across organisations, products, systems, and jurisdictions. It is not a legal, regulatory, accounting, or product-definition source.

## A–C

**Account servicing**

The activities performed after an account or facility is active, such as maintaining records, processing scheduled activity, responding to requests, and producing statements or notices.

**Approval hierarchy**

The sequence of roles or groups that may review, recommend, approve, or decline a request. The actual authority limits and delegation rules are organisation-specific.

**Audit trail**

A record of significant process events, such as who initiated, reviewed, changed, approved, released, or closed an item and when they did so.

**Authorizer**

A role that approves or declines an instruction after it has been prepared. In some organisations this role is called an approver or checker.

**Beneficial owner**

A person who ultimately owns or controls an entity or arrangement. The exact identification criteria vary by context and jurisdiction.

**Beneficiary / payee**

The intended recipient of a payment or transfer. A process may use one term or the other depending on the product.

**Collections**

Activities used to follow up on an overdue obligation and seek an appropriate resolution. The practical approach depends on the product, customer circumstances, and approved procedures.

**Core banking system**

A system of record that supports accounts, balances, customer records, and other banking operations. Its scope and interfaces differ by institution.

**Customer information file (CIF)**

A commonly used label for a customer profile or identifier that connects a customer to one or more accounts or products. Not every institution uses this name.

## D–H

**Debt service ratio (DSR)**

A measure that compares debt-payment commitments with income. The calculation inputs, limits, and decision use vary by product and organisation.

**Disbursement**

The release of approved funds to the intended recipient, such as a customer, seller, supplier, or other destination defined by the product.

**Enhanced review**

A more detailed review used when the normal automated or standard path is not sufficient. It may involve additional information, investigation, or a specialist decision.

**Exception**

An event, condition, or result that cannot proceed through the normal path and therefore requires retry, correction, review, escalation, hold, or closure.

**Facility**

A financing arrangement or approved line of credit. The term is often used instead of loan in commercial or institutional contexts.

**Four-eyes principle**

A working pattern in which one person prepares an item and another independent person reviews or authorises it. The design and applicability of this pattern must be set by the relevant owner.

## I–P

**Instruction**

A recorded request for a system or team to perform an action, such as opening an account, making a payment, changing a record, or releasing funds.

**KYC (know your customer)**

A shorthand for customer-identification and due-diligence activities carried out during onboarding and at other points in a relationship. The exact scope is context-dependent.

**Maker**

A role that creates, enters, or submits an instruction for subsequent review or approval.

**Onboarding**

The journey of establishing a customer, business, user, account, or service relationship, including the information collection and setup steps relevant to that relationship.

**Payment rail**

The network or arrangement through which a payment instruction is routed and settled. The available rails and their operating rules vary by market and product.

**Prescreening**

An early, limited assessment used to determine whether a request should proceed to fuller data collection or review.

## R–Z

**Reconciliation**

The comparison of expected records or outcomes with observed records or outcomes to identify and resolve differences.

**Risk rating**

A classification used to communicate the assessed level or type of risk associated with a customer, request, transaction, or relationship. Its methodology and consequences are organisation-specific.

**Sanctions screening**

A screening activity that compares relevant information with applicable lists or rules. The data sources, matching logic, escalation, and disposition process require specialist validation.

**Segregation of duties**

The deliberate separation of responsibilities so that one person or system does not control incompatible stages of a sensitive process without an appropriate independent check.

**Source of funds / source of wealth**

Terms used to describe the origin of particular funds or a person's overall wealth. The information requested and the review approach vary with the use case.

**Straight-through processing (STP)**

Processing that proceeds from input to outcome with little or no manual intervention once the required checks are satisfied. An exception path should still exist for failures or ambiguity.

**Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO)**

Another common term for the person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity or arrangement. See also **beneficial owner**.

## Using terms in diagrams

- Prefer a plain-language label when it is clearer than an acronym.
- Define an acronym on its first appearance in a companion guide.
- Do not imply that a generic term represents a named organisation's system, product, rule, or approval authority.
- Put jurisdiction-specific, legal, regulatory, product, or policy definitions in a reviewed source rather than this glossary.
